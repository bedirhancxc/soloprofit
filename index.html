Understand your quick-calculator results

These examples explain the assumptions behind the four calculators above. Replace the sample values to see how your own result changes.

Freelance rate: A $80,000 annual income goal plus $10,000 in yearly business costs, divided by 25 billable hours per week over 46 weeks, works out to $78.26 per billable hour before personal income tax. See the hourly-rate breakdown.

Project quote: A project taking 60 hours at $45 labor cost per hour, plus $500 of other costs, has a $3,200 cost base. Divide by (1 − 0.30) to aim for a 30% profit margin, giving a $4,571.43 quote. A margin is not the same as markup.

Subscription profit: For 350 paying customers at $29 per month, $4 direct cost each and $1,200 fixed monthly costs, the simplified monthly operating result is $7,550. Refunds, taxes and any unentered fees are excluded. Find subscription break-even.

Monthly profit target: To make $10,000 a month after $1,000 fixed costs, with $29 charged per customer and $4 variable cost each, you need at least 440 paying customers. The customer count is rounded up to a whole number.

What is included in these estimates?

Use each calculator to explore a pricing decision, then check the assumptions behind the result.

Costs you enter matter: Use monthly expenses for subscription estimates and annual expenses for freelance rates. Include relevant transaction fees, materials and other costs; the tools cannot know expenses you leave out.

Margin is different from markup: Margin divides profit by selling price, while markup divides profit by cost. Try the profit margin calculator and markup calculator with the same numbers to compare them.

Amounts are in USD. Results are estimates, not financial, tax or legal advice. Calculator inputs stay in your browser and no signup is required.
