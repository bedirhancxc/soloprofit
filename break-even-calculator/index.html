How to calculate the break-even point

Break-even units = fixed costs ÷ (selling price per unit − variable cost per unit). Round the result up to the next whole unit because you cannot normally sell part of a physical product. The difference between price and variable cost is the contribution margin per unit.

Break-even revenue = break-even units × selling price. This uses the rounded whole-unit threshold rather than a fractional theoretical sales level. Estimated operating profit = units sold × contribution margin per unit − fixed costs.

Break-even example

Imagine a shop pays $3,000 in fixed monthly costs. Each item sells for $50 and costs $20 to produce and fulfill. Each sale contributes $30 toward fixed costs. It takes 100 units to break even, equivalent to $5,000 in sales revenue. If the shop sells 150 units, estimated operating profit is $1,500.

Frequently asked questions

What if my variable cost is higher than my selling price?

Each extra sale increases your loss, so you cannot cover positive fixed costs with volume alone. Raise your price, reduce per-unit costs or change the business model.

What costs belong in fixed costs?

Include expenses that do not usually change with each additional sale during the period, such as rent, monthly software subscriptions and certain salaries. Treat payment fees or materials tied to each order as variable costs.

Does breaking even mean I have earned a profit?

Not necessarily. Breaking even means your modeled operating profit is zero or slightly above zero after rounding up whole units. Taxes, refunds and other unmodeled costs can still create a loss.

Can I use this calculator for a service business?

Yes. Treat one billable project, appointment or service package as a unit, using its price and direct delivery cost.
