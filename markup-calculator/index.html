Markup vs. margin

Markup is profit divided by cost. Margin is profit divided by selling price. A 50% markup on a $40 item produces a $60 price, $20 gross profit and a 33.33% gross margin.

Want a 50% margin instead?

For a $40 item, charge $80 to earn a 50% gross margin. A 50% markup would price it at only $60.

How do selling fees change your profit margin?

Suppose an item costs $40 and you apply a 50% markup. The selling price is $60, leaving $20 before other expenses. If the sale also incurs $6 in processing or marketplace fees, you keep $14 before other expenses: a 23.33% margin on the $60 sale. Include predictable per-item fees in the cost input when planning a new selling price, otherwise your estimate may be too high.

Frequently asked questions

Are markup and margin the same?

No. Markup is based on cost; margin is based on selling price.

Are fees and taxes included?

No. Include per-item shipping and fees in your cost. Check taxes separately.

Can I enter a $0 cost?

No. Enter a cost above $0 to calculate markup and margin.

Formulas

Price = cost × (1 + markup ÷ 100). Profit = price − cost. Margin (%) = profit ÷ price × 100.
