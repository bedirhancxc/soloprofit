Markup vs. margin

Markup is profit divided by cost. Margin is profit divided by selling price. A 50% markup on a $40 item produces a $60 price, $20 gross profit and a 33.33% gross margin.

Example: pricing a product for resale

Suppose you purchase a product for $40 and choose a 50% markup. Your selling price is $60, leaving $20 in gross profit before fees and other costs. The margin is $20 divided by $60, or 33.33%. To achieve a 50% margin on a $40 cost, you would instead need to charge $80.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between markup and margin?

Markup compares profit with cost; margin compares profit with selling price. They have different denominators, so their percentages are not interchangeable.

Does the calculator include sales tax or marketplace fees?

No. Add shipping, transaction fees, and other per-unit costs to the cost input if you want them reflected in your estimate. Taxes may require separate treatment.

What happens when cost is zero?

Enter a cost above $0. A percentage markup needs a positive cost.

Formulas

Selling price = cost × (1 + markup ÷ 100). Gross profit = selling price − cost. Gross margin (%) = gross profit ÷ selling price × 100.
